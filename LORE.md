# Isochronicle: Idle Realms - The Demon Lord's Return

## Core Premise
Centuries ago, the great Demon Lord was brought to the brink of true death by an alliance of human crusaders and their machine legions. His citadel was torn down to a floating island of rubble, his armies scattered, and he fell into a long slumber.

Now he has awakened. Weakened but resolute, he begins the long work of restoration: rebuild the citadel, raise a new horde of loyal monsters, and outlast the crusades that the Human & Mecha Alliance keeps sending against him.

## An Unlikely Ally
The Demon Lord wakes in the ruins of his throne room with only a fraction of his old magic. No generals answer him — only a single, enthusiastic **Slime**. That Slime becomes the **Slime Lord**, his first servant and the heart of the horde. It heals the wounded, restores tired minions, raises fallen ones back to their feet and cheers the army on with morale boosts. As it evolves through five forms, its healing reaches more allies and its blessings grow stronger.

## The Ancient Treant
The Slime's first act is to call a **Sprout Treant** out of the dead soil, free of charge. Stone by stone it raises the **Citadel**, then the **Crystal Spire**, waiting patiently whenever the stores run short. Then, as the mother of the realm, it calls forth the **Generals** one by one, in the order their homes are planned, until every one of the thirteen stands. It does not build the establishments: each General calls its household of minions one by one, and the minions raise the hall stone by stone from the castle stores. Once every General answers, it mends the castle walls, enriches the soil so harvests grow richer, and forges and buys gear for itself. It too evolves through five forms, from Sprout Treant to an ancient guardian of the grove.

## The Floating Island
The citadel stands at the centre of a floating island with the Crystal Spire beside it. No two islands share a shoreline: the sea bites its own coves and bays into every new realm, from calm curving shores to a shattered coast, and sandy beaches run wherever the grass meets the water. Around them the Treant raises thirteen establishments, placed anew in every realm: the **Wood Grove**, **Stone Quarry**, **Metal Mine**, **Water Port**, **Mystic Cave**, **Infernal Kennel**, **Brimstone Perch**, **Abyssal Trench**, **Crypt of Souls**, **Golem Foundry**, **Shadow Pavilion**, **Void Gate** and **Bone Crypt**. Each one is a home, a workshop and a fortress at once. Rank is earned: a minion whose deeds against the Sea Folk outshine its General's is raised to General, and the old General steps down to serve among the minions. It produces its own materials, guards the ground around it with its own tower, and calls on three skills of its own — two techniques and an ultimate. The Demon Lord may lift an establishment and set it down elsewhere, though never while an invasion is under way.

## Generals and Minions
Every establishment is home to one **General** and five **minions** of the same kind:

- the **Thornwood Dryad** of the Wood Grove, warden of the Tree of Life
- the **Watchtower Minotaur** of the Stone Quarry
- the **Ember Imp** of the Metal Mine and its ore furnace
- the **Water Merman** of the Port
- the **Lava Gargoyle** of the Mystic Cave, born of its magma
- the **Kraken** of the Abyssal Trench
- the **Lich Necromancer** of the Crypt of Souls
- the **Harpy** of the Brimstone Perch
- the **Demon Hound** of the Infernal Kennel
- the **Earth Golem** of the Golem Foundry
- the **Succubus** of the Shadow Pavilion
- the **Void Wraith** of the Void Gate
- the **Bone Knight** of the Bone Crypt

The Treant summons each General once the citadel and spire stand; the General's first task is to stand at its empty plot and call its minions, one at a time as the castle stores allow; the minions raise the establishment and later mend it, paying for every stone from the stores. After that it does not gather: it scouts the whole island, guards its home and the citadel, and leads in battle. The minions are the hands. Each kind works the ground that suits it. Mermen and Krakens wade into the ocean for fish, water and pearls. Dryads cut on the open grass and Minotaurs break stone from the paved roads. Imps, Golems, Harpies and Succubi work beside their own halls. Gargoyles, Liches and Wraiths draw essence from the air around the rifts. In war the minions garrison their home, and each one holds part of its defences; when the line breaks they tear free and strike back. A household is never left leaderless: when a General falls, the strongest of its minions still on the island takes up the mantle at once and becomes the new General, and a fresh minion later fills the empty place.

## The Citadel and Its Beacon
The rebuilt citadel no longer fights with its own hands. Its towers have gone quiet; the establishments do the killing now. Instead the citadel burns a **Provoke Beacon**, a taunting light that drags nearby invaders away from the establishments and onto the walls. Thick walls, a shield that soaks the first blows and a strong beacon keep the rest of the island safe.

## The Crusades
Once the citadel stands, the Human & Mecha Alliance notices. Every few minutes a new wave of invaders tears open the three **rifts** at the island's east, west and south corners and pours through them. Two **Rift Sentinel** towers guard every rift and shoot at any demon or beast that comes near; the minions must topple them before the rift itself can be struck. The rift fights back too, lashing nearby attackers, warding itself and its sentinels, and erupting once per wave when it is badly hurt. The Demon Lord's minions can smash an open rift: it stops spawning for the rest of the wave and pays a bounty, and once every rift is sealed no more invaders arrive. Some invaders are **rushers** that ignore everything and charge straight for the citadel. Between waves, lone **scouts** wander the island; striking one down yields easy loot. Slain invaders scatter materials on the ground for the minions to collect, and the Demon Lord can mark invaders for burning **lasers from the sky**, as many as ten at once. Each beam holds its prey, slowing it as it burns, and the longer it stays fixed on one foe, the likelier each burn lands as a critical blow. The scorched ground left behind keeps smouldering, burning and hobbling every invader that walks over it.

Not everything the horde needs grows on the island. Metal, scrap, souls and coin come from the human realm, so some minions walk into the rifts and gather on the other side, returning loaded a minute later. The humans notice. Every few raids, another of their own gatherers joins the next wave to take revenge, and that is why the crusades never stop coming. A raider caught across the rift when a wave begins waits there until the fighting is over.

The Alliance also attacks from the sky. Never in a wave, but sometimes when raiders slip through between waves, a **Kirov airship** drifts in over the island's edge: a great armoured crimson envelope with a lit gondola beneath. It opens its belly hatch and drops paratroopers under red-and-cream **parachutes**, then hovers over the citadel or an establishment and wrecks it with **Carpet Bombing**, a **Wrecking Anchor** swung down on its chain, and **Firebomb Rain** that leaves the ground burning. It ignores every fighter on the ground. Only flyers, long-range fighters, the towers and the Demon Lord's own strike can reach it, and its plating shrugs off almost half of every hit. Torn and burning, it limps away when its time runs out; brought down, it breaks apart in fire and the wreck is worth a fortune in coin. Archers loose arrows, priests and mages hurl glowing orbs, mecha fire bullets and plasma bolts, and siege engines lob stones that burst on impact; the horde's ranged fighters answer in kind with fireballs, thorns and dark magic. Each rift sits in the human den, wrapped in a cold fog where the lanterns of the Alliance's camp glow through; the fog parts when a wave begins or a scout slips through, and thins for anyone who peers into it. Human scouts carry **torches** and hurl them at establishments. A fire starts small, spreads into a full blaze if nobody acts, and brings the building down when it burns out of control, so the minions run a bucket brigade from the nearest shore until only smouldering ash is left.

If the citadel falls, the invaders loot half of everything in its stores and flee back through the rifts. The walls are raised again at once, and the war goes on.

## The Sea Folk
Beneath the island lives a third people who never wanted the war: the **Sea Folk**. Swarms of **Blood Piranha**, trident-singing **Tide Mermaids**, shelled **River Kappa**, and the one ancient **Abyssal Leviathan** have kept the sea floor in peace for ages. But every wave, every brawl, every burning establishment and every death spills ash, blood and runoff into their water, and little by little the sea turns foul. In calm times only their dark shapes drift under the waves. Once the poison grows too strong they rise from the shore whenever a battle starts and strike at demons and humans alike, for both sides are killing their home. The Mermaids heal their kin with the Tide Song, the Kappa hide in their shells, the Piranha frenzy at the scent of blood, and when the sea is near ruin the Leviathan itself surges up and crashes a tidal wave across the battlefield. Every life a Sea Folk warrior takes makes it stronger, and those who fall leave sea treasures on the shore: pearls, fish, water, stone and coin. Neither side takes it quietly: any demon or human the Sea Folk strike turns and fights back, calling the nearest comrades to its side, and whoever lands the killing blow, General or lowly minion alike, grows stronger for it. The Leviathan fights with three gifts of the deep: a **Tidal Wave** that crashes around it, an **Abyssal Roar** that slows every foe in a wide circle, and a **Maelstrom** that drags its enemies toward the shore. Its kin swear to guard it with their lives, hunting whoever strikes it and taking its blows on their own bodies. Even on a quieter tide the Leviathan may surface at random, and sometimes it rises with a rune from the sea floor that goes to whoever brings it down. The Demon Lord can cut them down, or offer a generous **tribute** that cleanses the water and buys a truce, during which they spare the demons and turn their anger on the humans alone.

## The Sand Brood
The Leviathan is not the only ancient pet of the Deities left on this island. Deep under the land burrows the **Ancient Caterpillar**, ruler of the ground, and with it a brood of **Dune Arachnids** that spit silk and **Sand Scorpions** that strike with venom. Every battle shakes the earth above them. Between battles the Caterpillar only moves below, unseen. But wherever it surfaces, in a wave or even in a random brawl, the ground heaves around it in a rounded swell: the sea there fills with sand, sand hardens back into land, and land is thrust up into **raised rock** that no walker or swimmer can cross, only flyers. The rock sinks back into land with the next dawn, and the sand settles into grass after a few days. When the ground grows restless the brood bursts out as a wave or a brawl begins, and it fights everyone: demons, humans and the Sea Folk alike. When the Caterpillar itself rises it strikes with a **Burrow Eruption** under its farthest foe, a **Silk Snare** that holds every enemy near it, and a **Sand Vortex** that drags them in and turns the land to sand, while its brood guards it with their lives. Like the Sea Folk, they can be slain for treasure and +, or calmed with a tribute.

The two ancient pets carve the island between them. Where the Leviathan fights, the shore turns to soaked, crumbling sand, and when it dies or sinks back into the deep that shore belongs to the sea for good. Where the Caterpillar surfaces, the land is heaved up and reborn. No two realms keep the same coastline for long.

## The Dragon's Lair
Where the North Rift once opened, a **volcano** now rises from the island's northern corner, and deep inside it sleeps **Ignara, the Ember Matriarch**, a dragon as old as the mountain. The first time the horde holds back five waves, the ground shakes and she wakes. From then on, after every fifth repelled wave, the cave at the volcano's foot glows open, and it stays open until the Demon Lord calls his expedition home or Ignara falls.

Every General goes in with its minions; the Treant and the Slime stay behind to keep the citadel. The cave's ward strips away whatever the horde borrowed: no blessings, no timed gifts, no powers of the Demon Lord's hand, and the + a General won in brawls is burned away for good. Only what was truly learned, research and runes, goes with them. The road is long and narrow, ten paces wide and eighty deep: a shelf at the entrance, galleries where **Giant Bats** dive for the stragglers at the rear, channels where magma pulses in and out, broken stone bridges the minions must mend, roosts of fire-spitting **Dragon Kin**, and a gate held by a **Magma Golem** that only opens when the Golem crumbles. Whoever falls on the road wakes again at the last place the expedition made camp.

In her chamber Ignara rises from her hoard, and at the same moment a **human expedition** breaks in through the volcano's far side, as many raiders as the horde has fighters left. No one is anyone's ally there: demons, humans and dragons all tear at each other. Ignara claws, bites, beats back the crowd with her wings and breathes long strips of fire; as she weakens she calls her brood, a swarm of bats, eruptions of magma under her foes and, at the last, a Magma Golem to stand over her. Her wounds never close. Whatever is taken from her stays taken, through every defeat, retreat and new day, so even a small expedition wears her down in time. Slain, she leaves a great hoard and a rune, though only half of it if the humans struck the last blows with no demon at her side. The kill is written into the realm's chronicle and the Hall of Legacy, and the cave seals until the next fifth wave, when a new Ignara claims the lair.

While Ignara lives, her volcano feeds the island too. It rumbles, the path ahead glows red, and **magma** spills down from its mouth onto the land, never onto the citadel, the halls, the roads or the rifts. Anything that stands in it burns, demon, human or wild creature alike. Slowly it cools to a dark crust and then to grey basalt, and where it settled on shore the sea had flooded, the basalt hardens into land again. To kill her is to win her hoard and silence the volcano; to let her live is to keep a cruel fire that also gives back what the sea has taken.

## The Invading Coalition
The Alliance is led by two **Rulers**: the **High Priest**, a holy healer who mends the soldiers around him, and the **Mecha Valkyrie**, the steel-winged commander of the machine legions. They lead the boss waves. Their eight **Fighters** are the frontline **Human Knight**, the sharp-eyed **Human Archer**, the fast **Mecha Scout**, the armoured **Mecha Titan**, the twin-bladed **Assassin**, swarms of laser-firing **Mecha Drones**, the long-range **Mecha Siege Tank**, and **Chrono**, the time mage who slows the battlefield around him.

## The Four Realms
The island can be anchored in one of four realms. The Demon Lord chooses where it rises when his reign begins, and he can drag it into another realm whenever he wishes:

- **Demon Citadel**: the ancestral seat of obsidian and aether spires.
- **Magma Caldera**: scorched basalt and rivers of molten fire.
- **Frost Spire**: glacial peaks and subzero storms.
- **Astral Sanctum**: a sanctuary of gold and white marble at the top of the heavens.

The realm changes the land, the waters and their creatures, but not the crusades: humanity sends the same ever-growing armies wherever the citadel stands.

## Seasons and Storms
A year in the realm lasts **365 days**, split into four seasons of about 91 days: a balanced Spring, a Summer of heatwaves, an Autumn of rain and storms, and a Winter of snow. The weather shapes every battle. Rain rusts the Mecha and slows them, snow chills the humans and slows them, and heatwaves enrage every invader so they hit harder. Some rainy days turn into thunderstorms that shake the island with lightning. On clear nights the sky fills with stars; in the deepest hours the old constellations show themselves — the Horned Crown, the Ancient Treant, the Slime and the Rift Serpent — while shooting stars streak past and, once in a long while, a slow comet crosses above the citadel.

## Relics of War
Five battle relics answer the Demon Lord's call, each fuelled by a rare material from the landmarks. **Minion Frenzy** fills the horde with burning fury, the **Aegis Barrier** makes the citadel and establishments untouchable for a short time while it refills the citadel's shield and patches every wall, **Mass Restoration** pours healing into every structure, the **Laser Storm** tears the sky open so the Demon Lord's lasers hunt up to ten invaders on their own, and **Chrono Surge** bends time so every skill is ready again. The Brimstone Perch forges obsidian, the Crypt gathers souls from every repelled wave, the Abyssal Trench yields pearls, and scrap is torn from fallen Mecha.

## Growing in Power
Victory itself feeds the horde. Every five waves cleared grants the Demon Lord new skill points to spend on his minions, his citadel, his harvests and his mystic arts. Research deepens what the Slime, the Treant, the citadel, the establishments and the minions can do, and the armory forges weapons, armour and relics for any minion.


## The Endless Crusade
The crusade has no last wave. After the hundredth assault the humans keep coming, and every twenty-fifth wave they march behind a champion of legend. There is no rewinding of time and no second life: if the citadel falls, the Demon Lord's reign ends for good, and only its memory remains, engraved in the **Hall of Legacy** — his name, the waves he held back, the days he endured and how long he ruled. The realm keeps its own chronicle after every wave, so a reign is never lost to anything but defeat.
